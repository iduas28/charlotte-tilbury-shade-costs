## What it actually is — and what it isn't

Hollywood Flawless Filter is not a foundation. Charlotte Tilbury describes it as a complexion booster, and the coverage level backs that up: sheer, illuminating, radiant finish. It sits somewhere between an illuminating primer and a liquid highlighter, which is exactly why people keep getting confused about where to put it in their routine.

The formula is water-based with Hydrogenated Didecene, mica, glycerin, propanediol and squalane, plus three ingredients the brand highlights:

- Smoothing airbrush polymers and lightweight glossy oil, for a poreless-looking finish
- Finely milled powders that blur the appearance of lines and pores
- Porcelain flower extract (Hoya Lacunosa, a Thai flower derivative) for a brightened look

The brand's published testing claims are specific enough to quote: moisturises skin for up to 24 hours (clinically tested on 30 people), and in a survey of 300 people tested over one week, 96% agreed it gives skin a real-life filter effect, 100% agreed skin looks glowing, and 99% agreed skin looks more youthful. Charlotte Tilbury also cites Circana Group UK retail tracking data for April 2023 to March 2024 to claim the #1 highlighter position in the UK prestige makeup market.

It's vegan and cruelty-free, free of parabens and SLS/SLES sulphates, and gluten-free. Sephora lists it among its Community Favorites, with close to a million "loves" on the product page alone — which tells you more about its cultural footprint than any award letter does.

## The four ways to wear it, and who each one suits

The official usage instructions list four methods. They produce genuinely different results, so it's worth matching one to your skin rather than trying all four and hoping.

**Alone, as a "no-makeup" base.** Apply with the wand, blend with fingers. You get sheer, fresh, glossy skin. This is the version that gets described as looking like you slept well. It's also the version that hides the least — hyperpigmentation, active breakouts and scarring will still be visible.

**Under foundation, as a glow primer.** Apply to the areas you want light, blend with a brush, then go in with foundation. This smooths the canvas and is the least risky option if you're nervous about the finish.

**Mixed with foundation.** One pump of each on the back of your hand, combined with a brush. The result is more dewy and all-over than either product alone. Good for an evening; potentially too much for a humid commute.

**On top of foundation, as a liquid highlighter.** Tap onto the tops of cheekbones, down the nose, on the cupid's bow. This is the most controlled way to use it, because you're using it as a highlighter rather than a base.

One caveat that comes up consistently in reviews: this is a dewy formula, and dewy is a short step away from shiny. If your skin is oily, you'll want to finish with a mattifying powder, and you may want to keep it off the sides of your nose entirely.

## The shade range: how to read the numbering

The full-size bottle comes in 12 shades. The mini comes in 8. Both use the same naming logic — a number followed by a depth word, from Fair through Light Medium, Medium, Tan and Deep. The official shade descriptions also name the undertone, which is the part that actually matters here: golden beige, warm peach, neutral peach, olive, warm amber, neutral bronze, deep bronze, warm mahogany.

Two practical points:

1. Because the coverage is sheer, shade mismatches are far more forgiving than they would be with a foundation. If you're between two, the lighter option usually reads better as a highlighter, the darker as a base.
2. Retailers don't all use the same product name on the listing. You'll see it labelled "Hollywood Flawless Filter", "Flawless Filter", and "Hollywood Flawless Filter complexion booster" depending on where you look. Same bottle, same 30 ml, different shelf labels.

If you're buying blind without a counter nearby, start from the undertone words rather than the number. The numbers move around between ranges; the undertone descriptors are what the brand uses to answer "is this pink, peach, olive or bronze".

## What third-party reviewers say

The Space NK editorial team tested the product across shades 3, 5 and 7. Their notes are more useful than the average review: the shade 5 tester said it's sheer enough that it won't cover hyperpigmentation on its own, but builds well under concealer or foundation. The shade 7 tester actually preferred it worn alone, like a tinted moisturiser, and found the finish less smooth when paired with a foundation. The team's summary flagged oily skin as the case needing a mattifying powder on top, and described the texture as creamy and blendable with no pilling.

Cosmopolitan's beauty writer, who has combination skin and wears shade 2.5 Fair, has used it for more than two years as an illuminating primer and specifically avoids the oilier areas of the face, noting that people with oily skin may want to skip it altogether. That's consistent with the Space NK findings rather than a one-off complaint.

The pattern across both: it's excellent as a glow layer, mediocre as a coverage product, and a risk on oily skin without powder.

## Who should skip it

Being clear about this saves people $50. This isn't for you if you want medium or full coverage, if you want a matte or soft-matte longwear finish, if your skin is genuinely oily and you dislike powder, or if the glow you're after is a highlighter-level beam rather than an all-over sheen. Shade 5 in the Space NK test and the Cosmopolitan writer both pointed at the same limitation — sheer coverage doesn't cover much, and it isn't meant to.

If what you actually want is coverage with some luminosity, the brand's Beautiful Skin Foundation is the more appropriate purchase, and it's the other half of the duo kit.
